#### Question 10: Disabled Parking

**Diane:** There's not enough disabled spaces in town. How are you going to create more and how are you going to police them?

* **Victoria Li:** My goal is to work with the next Constable. We need to be more proactive in designing and planning for more facilities for disabled access, especially with our aging population.
* **Kevin O'Connell:** As a disabled man myself with lymphedema, I know we need more disabled parking. There may be opportunities to rebuild certain car parks, especially in St Helier North. We also need to think about a well-managed transport network to reduce overall cars on the road, creating more space.
* **Gerald Voisin:** The parking issue has been ongoing since Gas Place was removed and Broad Street closed. We've got to provide better general parking in St Helier to get the town vibrant again. People come into town in cars. When we build better parking facilities, we will be able to provide better disabled parking alongside it.
* **Steve Ahier:** There is actually a plethora of disabled parking spaces at the moment, like on the ground floor of Sand Street, which are often not used. The Infrastructure Department has been working tirelessly to create more. However, we do need a new general car park in St Helier because the planning laws don't require developers to build parking spaces anymore.
* **Max Andrews:** You've got to commission work to identify how many disabled people are coming into town to work out capacity needs. It's an operational matter for the Minister for Infrastructure, engaging at a parish level. People have become more vocal about this, and we need to continually review progress.
* **Ian Barnes:** Overall, it's a traffic management problem. There is less street parking for everyone now. Crucially, the abuse of these parking spaces needs to be policed better against those without blue badges. Encouraging satellite parking out of town would also free up spaces for disabled parking.

#### Question 11: Cost of Living Measures

**Liz Levan:** Could you get specific on how you will get a handle on the cost of living?

* **Kevin O'Connell:** We're going to implement measures to reduce the burden on islanders, which includes looking at the cost of housing and removing GST from food and other essentials. This is detailed in our Reform Jersey manifesto.
* **Gerald Voisin:** The States of Jersey is a big driver of the cost of living. We must put every government policy through a filter: "Will this increase the cost of living?" If yes, we don't do it. Increases in duties all impact the cost of living. Secondly, we need the competition regulator to investigate why things like building costs are so enormous.
* **Steve Ahier:** Regarding GST on food, there is already a Community Cost Bonus designed to compensate low earners for that. Our current Retail Price Index is running lower than the UK. Specifically, I would not like to see impôt duty rises for one year, I would like a reduction in fuel duty, and we need to address freight costs to lower food prices. We also want a low-cost budget supermarket, but planning won't allow it.
* **Max Andrews:** We must rescind the flat-rate freight card with DFDS, which is inflationary, and return to volume-based discounts. Furthermore, government expenditure has embedded inflation in the economy. We must index payments so public sector pay awards aren't given above the rate of inflation, which causes local price spikes.
* **Ian Barnes:** We need faster housing approvals by cutting red tape to make housing affordable. To reduce pressure on everyday costs like food, we should closely review bringing in discount supermarkets from France, as we have close links with Normandy and Brittany.
* **Victoria Li:** Cost of living is several problems stacked together. Housing costs are worse than London. The RPI for low-income households is much higher than the headline rate. As a Deputy, I can't set prices, but I will scrutinize every major development decision, push for housing, and push for financial support for early years childcare to help young families.

#### Question 12: Artificial Intelligence & Jobs

**Iris Fritz:** With AI and automation increasing, middle-income admin jobs will fall away. How are you going to approach this transition so people aren't left unemployed and there isn't a hole in our income tax revenue?

* **Gerald Voisin:** To address the hole in taxes, we must grow the economy now by removing the risk for individuals to set up their own businesses. That dynamism keeps existing businesses competitive. There will be an onslaught of AI, but we must allow people to see opportunities to use AI to provide services, even off-island.
* **Steve Ahier:** AI won't necessarily cause mass unemployment; it often requires people to run it. If we build a data center, we would train and employ people for that. It would also require energy infrastructure, like offshore wind farms, creating more jobs. We also need more apprenticeship schemes for manual workers like plumbers and construction workers.
* **Max Andrews:** I created the Skills Development Fund precisely due to the impact of AI. Over 2,000 people have attended funded courses. It reviews skill gaps continuously to quickly redeploy unemployed people back into the labor market. I don't have concerns about personal income tax loss if we maintain this interventionist approach to retraining.
* **Ian Barnes:** AI is going to affect our businesses in the next two years, not five. The labor pool will increase. The only way to get those people re-employed is to encourage new businesses on the island by removing red tape so companies can establish themselves here more easily.
* **Victoria Li:** AI and automation have already started to shape our labor market. We need a digital skills strategy running from primary school to workforce retraining. We must treat digital literacy (coding, data literacy) as a foundational skill. For adults, we need accessible training programs, or our workforce will be disrupted without support.
* **Kevin O'Connell:** Language models are already building templates, and fields like law, politics, and education could face replacements soon. Reform Jersey would look at this through the lens of workers' rights. We support trade unions and would campaign for better conditions and retraining under a Reform Social Security Minister. We must also provide greater vocational training choices post-16.

#### Question 13: Diversifying the Economy for Youth

**Oscar:** What will you actually do to diversify the job industry to attract young people back to Jersey, and which industries specifically?

* **Steve Ahier:** Diversifying the economy and attracting young people back from big cities is very tricky. Most university students get fee subsidies, and it would be nice if they could give back to the island for a couple of years. But finding the right jobs outside of finance is an age-old dilemma we have to work out.
* **Max Andrews:** We need to focus on emerging industries like tech. It's a high-income, rewarding industry for young people. We need to be a pro-business government to see capital inflows and businesses relocating here. The deterrent right now is our tight labor market; businesses don't have the qualified labor available to them locally.
* **Ian Barnes:** Students have told us that to come back, they need subsidized student accommodation to feel established here given the housing costs. They also noted there isn't a lot to do here compared to city nightlife, so we need to encourage leisure developments like Fort Regent.
* **Victoria Li:** Our finance industry employs 40% of our workforce. I would like to do a skills audit so we understand our strengths rather than doing scattered investments. I would prioritize three industries: Green Finance, FinTech, and the Creative Economy.
* **Kevin O'Connell:** We are looking to deliver a new campus for Highlands College that creates a modern hub for further education connected to island life and industry. We can build on the industries we have, use vocational training, and utilize the expertise coming back to the island to create a more dynamic economy.
* **Gerald Voisin:** Governments don't diversify an economy; entrepreneurs do. We must rediscover our entrepreneurial spirit and allow businesses to grow. I see growth in the creative industries. We have fantastic optic fiber connections to France and the UK; there's no reason we can't have film-making, marketing, and digital trading based in Jersey servicing clients all over the world.
